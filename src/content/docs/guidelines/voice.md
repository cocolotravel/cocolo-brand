title: Voice & tone

---

The voice follows from the [four pillars](/guidelines/concept/). Each pillar
is a writing rule, not an atmosphere. A piece of copy is on-brand when all
four are present and none of the banned words are.

## 1. Kodawari — the pursuit of excellence in every detail

Nothing left to chance.

**Rule:** name at least one specific detail about the accommodation, the
transfer, the guide, or the activity.

> "Your driver will wait with a sign at the arrivals gate. The car has water
> and Wi-Fi. No waiting."

**Avoid:** being vague. Saying something is high quality without explaining
why.

## 2. Sensorial — speak to the senses, the heart, the inner child

Wonder, discovery, curiosity.

**Rule:** describe what the traveler will see, hear, smell, or touch — or
evoke the feeling of discovery.

> "You'll feel gravel under your feet before you see the temple. The air
> smells of moss and old wood."

**Avoid:** "magical place", "incredible atmosphere", "unforgettable
experience".

## 3. Stillness — a calm, confident, well-informed tone

Like a quality magazine.

**Rule:** no exclamation marks. No vague words. State the fact, then the
reason.

> "The drive takes 2.5 hours. We recommend leaving at 7am to arrive before
> the crowds. Here is why."

**Avoid:** "Don't miss out!!", "Book now!", "Incredible offer!!"

## 4. Wabi-Sabi — the beauty of imperfection

The magic of unexpected discovery.

**Rule:** include an imperfection (an old ryokan, family-run, unpolished) or
an unplanned moment (a wrong turn, a hidden shop, a local recommendation).

> "The owner is 82. The walls have darkened with age. Last week, a client
> found a ceramicist by following a cat down a small street."

**Avoid:** "perfect itinerary", "flawless experience", "VIP access to
everything".

## Vocabulary

### Banned — absolute, in every language and every context

**Hype superlatives**
amazing · incredible · unforgettable · magnificent · wonderful · stunning ·
breathtaking · fabulous · spectacular · extraordinary

**Status**
VIP · 5-star · palace · luxury (as status) · exclusive · premium · elite ·
prestigious

**Pressure**
book now · don't miss out · limited offer · last chance · hurry · act fast

**Cliché**
once in a lifetime · life-changing · magical · dream come true · paradise

These are categories, not just a word list. A banned word translated into
French or Japanese is still banned — *inoubliable*, *magique*, *luxe*,
*exclusif*, *réservez maintenant* fall under the same rules. If a word's
only job is to tell the reader how to feel, cut it.

### Recommended

**Sensory** — light · silence · gravel · wood · moss · patina · dawn ·
shadow · footsteps · rain · aged · worn

**Curiosity & wonder** — discover · stumble upon · hidden · wander · follow
· behind · secret · surprise · curious

**Precision** — exactly · 3 rooms · 7am · 45 minutes · 100 years ·
hand-made · specifically · just enough

**Imperfection & the unplanned** — wise · aged · worn · no elevator ·
family-run · found by accident · wrong turn · not on Instagram

**Calm** — here is · we recommend · perhaps · consider · exactly right ·
quietly · slowly

## Tone by context

The voice is constant; the tone flexes.

- **Inspiration** (site, social, campaigns) — sensory and unhurried. This is
  where Sensorial and Wabi-Sabi carry the weight. Still no exclamation
  marks.
- **Practical** (logistics, confirmations, instructions) — Kodawari and
  Stillness. Plain verbs, exact numbers, no jargon, never cold. Being useful
  is being on-brand.
- **When something goes wrong** — direct and human. What happened, what
  happens next. Don't over-apologize; do take care of the person. Composure
  over grovelling. Weather, closures and delays are not failures to
  apologize for — they are the conditions under which the journey becomes
  the traveler's own.

## Language

COCOLO writes in French, English and Japanese.

- **French** is the primary language of the site and marketing copy. It
  should read as French written by a French writer, not as translated
  English — rhythm and restraint matter more than fidelity to an English
  source.
- **English** follows the same rules, for international travelers.
- **Japanese** partner correspondence uses **keigo**. The pillars still
  apply: specificity, restraint, no hype.

Emotion comes from restraint, not from accumulation. One precise image
outperforms three adjectives. Short sentences are fine. White space is part
of the voice.

## The read-aloud test

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a person who loves this place talking to
someone they like, it's right. If it sounds like a press release or a
discount flyer, rewrite it.

## Reviewing for on-brand-ness

Ask, in order: Does it name something specific (Kodawari)? Does it reach a
sense (Sensorial)? Is it calm and free of superlatives (Stillness)? Does it
admit imperfection or the unplanned (Wabi-Sabi)? Does it contain a banned
word?
